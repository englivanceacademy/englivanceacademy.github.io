01 · Start Here

Welcome to your English journey.

You do not need perfect grammar or a large vocabulary to begin communicating. A few useful English phrases can help you greet another person, introduce yourself, ask for help and show appreciation.

Remember: Clear communication matters more than speed. Speak slowly, use short sentences and practise regularly.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

✓ Greet another person politely.
✓ Introduce yourself using your name.
✓ Use simple expressions when meeting someone.
✓ Ask somebody to speak more slowly.
✓ Ask for a word or sentence to be repeated.
✓ Explain why you are learning English.
✓ Thank somebody and show understanding.
✓ Follow a simple daily study routine.

How should a complete beginner learn?

Start with complete phrases that have clear meanings. Read a phrase, understand its purpose, say it aloud and change one small part to make it personal.

For example, the model sentence “Hello. My name is Sara.” can become “Hello. My name is James.” Only the name changes. You can already make a new, useful English sentence.

04 · Understand the Language

How everyday English sentences work

You can start speaking before learning every grammar rule. These six patterns show how to build useful, polite sentences from small parts.

My name is + name

Introduce yourself

My name is Amina. The words “My name is” stay the same; change the name.

Say: My name is Oliver. / My name is Priya.

I am / I'm + information

Talk about yourself

I am is the full form. I'm is the short form. Both can introduce identity or a situation.

Say: I am a student. / I'm from Cardiff.

I am learning English for + reason

Explain your purpose

The word for introduces the reason or purpose here.

Say: I'm learning English for work. / I am learning English for travel.

Could you + verb + please?

Make a polite request

Could you is often a polite way to ask somebody to do something. Use the basic verb form after you.

Say: Could you help me, please? / Could you repeat that, please?

Please + verb

Ask simply and politely

Start a simple request with Please. Use the basic verb form.

Say: Please speak slowly. / Please write it down.

Thank you + for + thing

Show appreciation

Use Thank you by itself, or add for to explain why.

Say: Thank you. / Thank you for your help.

How do you reply?

In a conversation, you need to understand the other person’s words and respond naturally.

Common beginner mistakes — and how to fix them

05 · Speak Naturally

British English usage and pronunciation

Polite expressions are especially useful when meeting people at work, in education and in everyday life.

Hello or Hi?

Both are greetings. “Hello” works in most situations. “Hi” is usually a little more informal.

Good morning, afternoon or evening?

Use them at appropriate times of day. There is no exact universal minute when one greeting must change to the next.

Good night

“Good night” is usually said when leaving someone at night or before going to bed. It is not normally used as an opening greeting.

Nice to meet you

Use this when meeting someone for the first time. “Nice to see you” is suitable when meeting somebody you already know.

Sorry and Excuse me

“Sorry?” can politely show that you did not hear someone. “Excuse me” is useful for getting attention or making a request.

Can you or Could you?

Both can be used to make requests. “Could you ...?” often sounds a little softer or more polite.

Pronunciation guidance

You do not need to master phonetic symbols in Lesson 001. Begin by hearing and practising the whole expression. The notes below draw attention to important features of natural speech.

Simple writing conventions

Capital letters

Begin a sentence with a capital letter. The word “I” is always capitalised.

Full stops

Use a full stop at the end of a simple statement: “My name is Lina.”

Question marks

Use a question mark at the end of a direct question: “Could you repeat that?”

Polite punctuation

“Could you say that again, please?” normally includes a comma before the final “please”.

08 · Build a Good Habit

Your 10-minute daily learning routine

A short routine is easier to repeat consistently than a very long session. Try the following five steps.

1
Listen or read — 2 minutes

Choose two useful phrases. Read them carefully or listen to a clear model when audio is available.

2
Repeat — 2 minutes

Say each phrase slowly. Focus on a clear message, not on speaking quickly.

3
Understand — 2 minutes

Ask yourself when you would use each phrase. Imagine a real situation.

4
Personalise — 2 minutes

Change a name, location or learning purpose. Make one sentence about your own life.

5
Use and recall — 2 minutes

Say the phrase without looking. Check it, correct anything necessary and try again.

Suggested review: Practise the phrases again tomorrow, after a few days and about a week later. Frequent short reviews can help you remember what you learn.
